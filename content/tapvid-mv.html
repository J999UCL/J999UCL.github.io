I co-authored TAPVid-MV, a benchmark for tracking a physical point in 3D across several synchronized videos. The cameras can move independently, and the point can become hidden in one view while remaining visible in another.

The benchmark

The release contains 284 sequences, 1,142 camera streams and 109,769 ground-truth trajectories across seven subsets. They cover robotics, human activity, driving and procedural scenes. Evaluation considers the query camera, other cameras and a shared world frame, alongside reconstruction quality.

This distinction matters: a poor 3D trajectory can come from incorrect correspondence, inaccurate geometry, or both. Evaluating tracking and reconstruction together helps separate these effects.

My contribution

My work focused on Perpetua’s synthetic-data pipeline and fine-tuning MVTracker. Those pages describe the implementation, training recipe, and results.
